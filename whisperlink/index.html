01

What ATLAS WhisperLink Is

ATLAS WhisperLink is a serverless, peer-to-peer, end-to-end encrypted messenger for Android and iPhone. Each phone is its own server: messages, voice notes, and calls travel directly between your device and your contact's device over the Tor network as hidden-service traffic. We hold none of your messages, keys, or contacts. Two things do not always travel directly: instant delivery to iPhones (Section 05) and video and group calls (Section 08).

The design principle behind this policy

Most privacy policies describe what a company does with your data. Ours is shorter, because the app is built so that there is no company in a position to read your messages or learn who you talk to. What follows is an honest accounting of what stays on your phone, what crosses the network, and exactly what our two helper services (the doorbell relay and the video relay) and Apple can and cannot see.

Where Your Data Lives

Everything the app knows lives on your phone:

Encrypted local database

Messages, contacts, and voice notes are stored in a database encrypted with SQLCipher (AES-256). The database key is wrapped by a key held in your phone's hardware security module (StrongBox or TEE) — it never exists in plain form on disk.

Cryptographic keys

Your identity keys are generated on your phone and never leave it. Session secrets are derived per contact via an X3DH handshake and per-message keys via modern authenticated encryption (ChaCha20-Poly1305, Ed25519, X25519, BLAKE2b — libsodium).

Panic wipe

A single action destroys the database, all keys, Tor state, and scheduled work, then terminates the app. There is no recovery, no backup, and no copy anywhere else — by design.

04

How Messages Travel

Each phone runs a Tor hidden service. Sending a message means your phone dials your contact's onion address through the Tor network and delivers the encrypted message directly to their phone.

  • End-to-end encrypted: only your contact's device can decrypt. Tor relays carry ciphertext inside Tor's own encryption layers.
  • Waits on your phone: if your contact's phone is unreachable, the message waits on your phone and retries. Our servers never store it. A text message to an iPhone can also travel as an encrypted push, which Apple may hold until the iPhone is reachable (Section 05).
  • Anonymous transport: Tor hides each phone's IP address from the network and from intermediate relays.
  • Same Wi-Fi: two contacts on the same Wi-Fi connect directly instead, still end-to-end encrypted. Others on that Wi-Fi can see that a WhisperLink phone is present, not whose.
  • Man-in-the-middle defense: every pairing produces a safety number both people compare in person or by voice. Remotely-paired contacts stay quarantined until verified.
05

The Doorbell Relay — Honest Accounting

Keeping Tor running 24/7 costs battery. The optional doorbell lets your phone sleep: each phone keeps one tiny connection to a relay server, and when someone has a message for you, the relay rings your phone — which wakes its own Tor and fetches the message directly from the sender. The relay is a doorbell, not a mailbox: it stores no messages and cannot read the messages it carries.

When your phone is connected (online/offline schedule) Who rang you — rings arrive through Tor, so senders are anonymous
Ring times, whether a ring is for a call or a message, and when an encrypted push is handed to Apple for an iPhone What any message says, or who sent it
A sealed (encrypted) hint blob it cannot open Your contact list, onion addresses, or keys
Your IP address when your phone sets up a video or group call Who you call, or anything said or shown in a call

What the relay keeps

The relay keeps no messages. The only thing it saves to disk is the iPhone push-token file described below; its log holds timed event lines only (for example “a push was sent”), never a token, an address, or a message. Its working memory: which tokens are connected, rate-limits (which can hold an IP address for up to an hour), and (for at most 10 minutes) the latest missed ring per token so a phone that was briefly offline still gets woken. A relay restart erases that memory and phones simply reconnect.

Your choice, three ways

The app ships pointing at our relay so wake-ups work out of the box. You can replace it with your own self-hosted relay (it wakes Android phones only; waking an iPhone needs ours), or turn the doorbell off entirely — on Android, Tor then stays connected all the time, which uses more battery; on iPhone, messages then wait until you and your contact next connect (whether your iPhone can be woken is the separate Wake via Apple switch). Video and group calls still use the video relay (Section 08) unless you set your own.

On iPhone — Wake via Apple

iOS does not let an app keep its own doorbell connection open while the phone sleeps. With Wake via Apple on — it is on by default in the App Store version, and you can turn it off in Settings — the app gives the relay an Apple push token so the relay can ask Apple to wake your phone when someone has a message or a call for you. Apple knows which device a token belongs to; our relay sees only the token and the IP address it came from, and does not save the address. The relay keeps the token in one file, tied only to your random doorbell token (which rotates about weekly), for as long as your phone stays registered. Turning the setting off removes it; otherwise it is removed when Apple reports the token dead, or about 30 days after the phone last registered. It is never written to a log and never linked to a name or a contact.

Instant Delivery to iPhones

A sleeping iPhone cannot fetch a message by itself in time. So when Wake via Apple is on, the sender's phone (Android or iPhone) also sends an encrypted copy of a text message through our relay to Apple's push service, and the iPhone shows it at once. An incoming-call alert travels the same way, so the iPhone can show who is calling. Only the receiving iPhone can open it: our relay and Apple cannot read it and cannot see who sent it. Our relay passes it on at once and stores nothing. We ask Apple to drop it after 24 hours if the iPhone has not come online. Both can see that something was sent to that iPhone, when, and how often. The iPhone keeps the shown banner in its notification list until you clear it. The message itself still travels phone to phone as well. Photos, voice notes, files, very long texts, disappearing messages and group messages never travel this way. This follows the receiver's setting: turning Wake via Apple off unregisters that iPhone with Apple and our relay, so no further copies reach it. To keep your own texts off this path, turn the doorbell off on your phone.

06

Offline Mode — Bluetooth, Wi-Fi Direct, and Hotspots

Offline Mode (off by default) lets two phones in the same place message and call each other with no internet at all — no Tor, no relay, nothing leaves the room.

What your phone broadcasts. While the Offline tab is open, your phone announces a rotating set of pairwise recognition tags: each tag is computed from a secret you share with one specific contact and changes every 15 minutes. Only that contact can recognise their tag; to anyone else the broadcast is random-looking noise and identifies nobody. The broadcast also carries a random name that changes with the tags. Your chosen display name is broadcast only while the "Visible to people nearby" switch is on, so that someone who is not yet a contact can ask to connect.

When the radios run. Bluetooth and Wi-Fi Direct are used only while the Offline tab is open on your screen. Nothing runs in the background, and turning Offline Mode off stops every radio immediately. One exception: a hotspot link that is already carrying a conversation stays open if you switch to another tab or leave the app, so the conversation is not cut off. It still closes about two minutes after the conversation goes quiet, and always within 30 minutes.

Meeting someone new. A stranger appears on your radar only while both of you are Visible. Connecting needs an explicit accept on both phones, and the two of you must compare a safety number in person before the chat opens — the same trust ceremony as every other pairing.

How the link is carried. Android phones talk to each other directly over Wi-Fi Direct. Two iPhones link directly, phone to phone, over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth. To talk to an iPhone, an Android phone opens a short-lived private hotspot (no internet on it) and hands the iPhone its credentials over an encrypted Bluetooth exchange; the iPhone joins and the ordinary end-to-end encrypted session runs over that link. The hotspot closes itself after a couple of minutes without a live conversation, and always within 30 minutes.

What this reveals. Someone scanning nearby can tell that a WhisperLink phone is present (not whose), and a contact you are linked to over a hotspot shares that small private network with you for the duration. Nothing about Offline Mode is sent to the internet, to us, or to the relay.

Subscription

ATLAS WhisperLink is a paid subscription, billed by Google Play or the App Store. The store handles payment and your identity; we receive no payment details, no name, and no account — the app only learns from the store whether a subscription is active, and remembers that answer on the phone so it keeps working for a while offline. On iPhone the app checks Apple's signed entitlement record on the device; on Android it asks Google Play Billing on the device. When a subscription ends, the app stops sending and receiving messages until it is renewed. Cancellation and refunds are handled by the store. You can check your status, manage or cancel, and restore a purchase at any time from Settings → Subscription in the app.

08

Voice & Video Calls

Voice calls

One-to-one voice calls travel over Tor between the two phones, end-to-end encrypted with the same message crypto. The ring that announces a call goes through the doorbell (Section 05). Group calls work like video calls.

Video calls

Use WebRTC with DTLS-SRTP end-to-end encryption, with key fingerprints exchanged over the Tor-encrypted channel — a relay can never decrypt the call. When both phones are on the same network, video flows directly. Across networks, encrypted packets may traverse a TURN relay (ours, or one you host): that relay sees encrypted packets and the two IP addresses, never content or keys. In a video or group call the other person's phone may learn your IP address; turn on Always use the relay for video calls to hide it from them (the relay then sees it instead). Call signaling travels phone to phone over the same encrypted channel as messages.

Your Controls

  • Verify safety numbers — the trust ceremony that rules out interception; unverified remote pairings stay quarantined.
  • Per-contact delete — removes the contact and the entire conversation from your phone.
  • Doorbell toggle + relay override — use ours, use yours, or none.
  • Block — a blocked person cannot message you, call you, reach you through a shared group, or pair with you again. Blocking happens entirely on your phone and is instant; unblocking restores the conversation.
  • Panic wipe — one action, everything gone, no recovery.
